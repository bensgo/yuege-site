How to Back Up and Restore Encrypted Photos on iPhone

To back up encrypted photos on iPhone, export a compatible encrypted vault file, keep its required PIN, then use the app’s restore flow and verify the result. In Hide Photos, the export covers the currently open vault; restoring adds missing items to the destination after confirmation. Test this before erasing your old phone.

Written by the Hide Photos developer · Yuege Apps

Why normal photo sync may not be enough

iCloud Photos manages media stored in your Photos library. A private vault may hold a separate encrypted copy inside its own app storage. That copy may not appear in Apple Photos or migrate the same way. Never assume the vault is synchronized just because the rest of your photo library is.

A five-step checklist

  1. Update and unlock your old vault. Confirm you can still access the important photos and videos.
  2. Create an encrypted export. Use the app's backup or export function, if available. Save the backup file somewhere you control, such as Files, an external drive, or a chosen file provider.
  3. Keep the correct credentials. A portable encrypted backup may require its original PIN or recovery information. Face ID on a new iPhone is not a replacement for that secret.
  4. Restore on the new device. Install the vault app and choose its supported import or restore flow. UI wording varies by version.
  5. Verify before wiping the old phone. Check a few photos, videos, albums and notes, including large files. Keep a second backup until you're confident everything transferred.

Hide Photos: Secret Album backup approach

Hide Photos: Secret Album supports a user-controlled encrypted backup file intended to be restored using the correct PIN. The developer does not run a cloud service that can recover your vault contents for you. Check your installed version and plan for the backup limits or Pro features available to you.

For details on how encryption and portability are designed, read the Hide Photos: Secret Album security page.

Common transfer mistakes

  • Deleting the old app before confirming a working backup.
  • Forgetting the backup PIN or assuming Face ID alone is enough.
  • Exporting only thumbnails or a subset of albums.
  • Placing the backup in a location you can't access on the new iPhone.
  • Confusing an encrypted vault backup with a regular iCloud Photos sync.

What if you've already deleted the app?

Recovery depends on whether you have a compatible backup and the required credentials. A privacy-focused developer who does not hold your keys generally cannot regenerate lost encrypted files. Avoid any recovery service that promises guaranteed access without knowing how the encryption works.

Create an encrypted backup in Hide Photos

The Backup & Restore screen explains that Hide Photos does not upload your backup to the developer. You decide where the exported file is stored. The screenshots below show the English interface, including the free/Pro backup limits visible in this version.

Restore an encrypted backup: a real three-step flow

  1. Open Backup & Restore in the destination vault and select Restore Encrypted Backup.
  2. Choose the exported file and enter the backup PIN to validate it.
  3. Review the merge confirmation. The app keeps your current local data and PIN, and adds items missing from the current vault after you confirm.

Frequently asked questions

Does a backup include all three decoy vaults automatically?

No. The current screen explicitly states that a backup includes only the vault you are using. To back up multiple vaults, unlock and export each one separately, retaining the corresponding PIN.

Does restoring a backup erase my current vault?

The app's restore confirmation says existing local data and the current vault PIN remain unchanged. Missing items from the backup are added to the destination vault after validation and user confirmation. Test a small backup before relying on any merge behavior.

Is restoring an encrypted backup free?

Yes. The supplied app screen says restoring is always free. That screen also states that Free includes one encrypted backup and Pro offers unlimited backup creation; check the current installed version and plan.

Can the developer recover my backup if I forget the PIN?

No. The app states that Hide Photos cannot recover a backup if you forget its PIN. Keep credentials somewhere safe and test a restore before deleting originals.

Does this automatically sync my vault across devices?

No. A local encrypted export is a manual backup and transfer workflow, not ongoing synchronization of your vault between devices.

Will my original copies in Apple Photos disappear after exporting?

No. Making a vault backup does not remove originals from Apple Photos, Recently Deleted, iCloud Photos or other backups. Review all copies separately.

Will restoring replace my destination vault's PIN?

No. The currently displayed merge confirmation says the existing vault PIN stays unchanged.

If a backup or restore does not finish

Keep the source vault and exported file intact. Check available device storage and whether the file is fully available locally rather than still downloading from a file provider. Try a small sample backup to distinguish a file-access problem from a credential problem. If the PIN is rejected, use the PIN required by that backup; changing the destination PIN does not unlock an older export.

After restoring, open photos at full size and play videos, rather than relying only on thumbnail visibility. If anything is missing, retain the old phone and contact support before deleting or reinstalling either app. Do not send private media or your PIN with a support request.
